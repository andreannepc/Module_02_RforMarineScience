### Interpretation ####The grouped analysis demonstrates how individual morphological observations can be transformed into population-level summaries. Comparing mean body mass and variation among species and islands makes differences between biological groups easier to identify than examining the raw observations alone. The exercise also demonstrates that the choice of uncertainty measure matters: standard deviation describes variation among individuals, whereas standard error describes the precision of the estimated group mean.

Interpretation

The final visualisation integrates biological catch data with environmental measurements collected along the estuary. Faceting by species allows species-specific abundance patterns to be compared across the salinity gradient, while inclusion of explicit zero catches ensures that observed absences contribute to the ecological interpretation rather than being omitted from the analysis.

###Troubleshooting During the workflow, all temperature and salinity values initially appeared as missing after joining the biological and environmental datasets. I traced the problem backwards through the pipeline and found that all 11,520 sonde timestamps had been converted to NA because the original timestamps contained hours and minutes but no seconds. Replacing dmy_hms() with the appropriate dmy_hm() parser corrected the timestamps and allowed the datasets to join successfully. This demonstrated the importance of validating intermediate outputs rather than assuming that code has worked simply because it runs without an error.
